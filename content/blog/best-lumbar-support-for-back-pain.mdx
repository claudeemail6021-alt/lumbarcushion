Lower back pain is the leading cause of disability worldwide. More than 80% of people will experience it at some point in their lives, and for many, prolonged sitting is the primary trigger. If your back hurts when you sit, where and how you sit matters enormously.

A quality lumbar support cushion is often the single most effective intervention for sitting-related lower back pain. It works by filling the gap between your lower back and the chair, supporting your natural lumbar curve, and reducing the muscle and disc load that causes pain to develop and worsen over a long day.

## How Sitting Causes Lower Back Pain

When you sit without lumbar support, your pelvis gradually tilts backward. This flattens the natural S-curve of your lumbar spine and shifts your weight onto the posterior edges of your lumbar discs. Over hours, this posture compresses the discs unevenly, fatigues the paraspinal muscles, and irritates the facet joints.

The result is the dull aching pain most desk workers know well — often starting in the lower back, sometimes radiating into the hips or glutes, and worsening through the afternoon.

A lumbar cushion prevents this by gently holding the lumbar region in its natural forward curve. Your muscles can relax, your discs are loaded more evenly, and your facet joints are not compressed.

## How to Use a Lumbar Cushion Correctly

Position matters as much as the cushion itself. The cushion should support the natural inward curve of your lower back — not your mid-back and not your tailbone. Position it roughly at belt-line height.

When positioned correctly, you should feel gentle pressure pushing your lower back forward. If you feel pressure at your shoulder blades or tailbone, reposition the cushion lower or higher respectively.

Your chair's seat depth also matters. If you are sitting with a large gap between the back of your knees and the seat edge, scoot forward until your knees have 2–3 finger widths of clearance from the seat, and then reposition the lumbar cushion.

## When Lumbar Support Is Not Enough

A lumbar cushion is highly effective for posture-related back pain but it is not a cure for all types of back pain. If your pain is severe, getting worse over time, associated with leg weakness or numbness, or if you have had trauma to your spine, see a doctor.

For most desk workers with posture-related lower back pain, however, a good lumbar cushion combined with movement breaks every hour will produce a noticeable improvement within days.
